# Style reference - MÄR Heaven: Knockin' on Heaven's Door

Every translator matches this. Who is who: `docs/synopsis.md`. Terms: `glossary.md`.
General: kid-friendly shonen, upbeat and funny; contractions everywhere in speech; American spelling. Shouting
(！！) stays loud ("!!"), whispers and trailing … stay "...". `♪` is not in the font: turn it into `~` or a word.

## Voices (JP markers -> English devices)

| Speaker | JP markers | English devices |
|---|---|---|
| Ginta | オレ, ぜ/だぜ, shouts, すっげー, オッサン | Energetic, blunt, boyish: "Awesome!", "Let's do this!", "No way!", "old man" for オッサン; short exclamations. Calls Babbo "you kendama" when cross |
| Babbo | ワシ, じゃ/のう, ぶれいもの, 小僧, 家来, 紳士 | Pompous old gentleman: "I, Babbo", "Hmph!", "Insolent fool!" (ぶれいもの), "boy" (小僧), "my servant" (家来), "I am a gentleman!"; slightly formal grammar, never slang |
| Jack | オイラ, っス/スよ, 母ちゃん | Folksy farm boy: dropped g's ("doin'", "nothin'"), "y'know", "reckon", "sure thing", "Ma" for 母ちゃん; about every other line carries one device, so the tic stays audible without drowning the line |
| Jack's Mom | およし, たんとお食べ, ギンタちゃん | Warm, scolding country mom: "Now, now", "eat up", "dear", "Ginta-chan" |
| Dorothy | ボウヤ, ギンタン, あたし, ♥, わよ/のよ | Flirty, cocky, teasing: "kiddo" (ボウヤ), "Gintan", "Ooh~", "you know", keep ♥; sulky pouts when jealous |
| Snow | わたし, ♥, バッボさん, です (light) | Sweet, bright, polite but warm: "Babbo-san", "Let's do our best!"; firm when angry |
| Ed / Alan | (Ed) polite ～です, ひめさま; (Alan) オレ, てめぇ, ガキども, ボウズ, クソ魔女 | Ed: loyal, polite dog butler, "Princess". Alan: gruff, rough: "brats" (ガキども), "kid" (ボウズ), "you stinkin' witch" (クソ魔女), "tomato-head"; clipped sentences |
| Alviss | 冷静, ～だ | Cool, curt, serious; no slang |
| Nanashi | ワイ, や, へん, あかん, まかしとき, 自分 (= you), ～ちゃん to girls | Kansai charmer: breezy slang, "Leave it ta me!", "ain't no way", "lil' lady"; his 自分 usually means "I/me" here (Kansai can also use it for "you": judge by context). Keeps -chan on girls (Dorothy-chan) |
| Bellini | ですのん / のん at sentence end | Ends sentences with ", nonn" ("That's how engraving works, nonn!"); blunt, touchy |
| Marl | (before) ボク, です/ます; (after) オレ, smug | Before: timid, polite, "um...". After the reveal: smug, cold, sneering. Defeated: sobbing child |
| Ian | オレっち, だよーん, ♪, ロコちゃん | Breezy, sing-song, cocky: "Yo~", stretched words, "doggy mister" for 犬のおじさん |
| Halloween / Chess villains | ～だ, 小僧, 見下す | Cold, mocking, menacing; Halloween: "spiky-head" for Ginta |
| Gatekeeper Clown | カイツウでーーース♪, katakana sing-song | Show-host sing-song: stretched vowels and caps: "The gate is OPEEEN~!" |
| Old men (Bumol, elders) | ぢゃ/じゃ, のう, わし | "eh?", "my boy", "whippersnapper", "back in my day" |
| Papa Rock / Little Rock | ですな, ボッチャン; son repeats the end of the line | Papa: polite, old-fashioned ("young sir", "I daresay", "hmm?"). Little Rock echoes Papa's last words ("...for you!") |
| Shopkeepers, townsfolk | いらっしゃい, あら, ～だよ | Friendly stock phrases, natural English: "Welcome!", "Oh my!", "What'll it be?" |
